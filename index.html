Can a fitness tracker warn you before you burn out?

The data had no "burnout" column, so we had to define one. We flagged any day where a person's 7-day average steps fell below 70% of their own normal. Then we predicted those days using only what happened before them, so the model works like a real early warning.

21 of 30decline cases caught on unseen people
0.592F1, best of three models
28 / 7people in train / test, no overlap

What we did

  • Rolled hourly intensity up to daily totals and joined it to daily activity and sleep with left joins, keeping all 1,373 days.
  • Removed a duplicate measure (two distance columns 99% correlated) and left sleep out of the final model, since only 24 of 35 people had any.
  • Built three backward-looking features: prior 7-day intensity, change in sedentary minutes, and step trend.
  • Split by person, not by row, so the model could not cheat by memorizing someone's habits.
  • Tuned the tree with GridSearchCV. The tuned version scored lower on the test set (F1 0.575), so we kept the original.

What gives a fake job posting away?

Only about 1 in 20 postings in this dataset is a scam, so the signals are easy to miss. I cleaned and profiled every column, engineered a "has company profile" flag, and compared fraud rates across the features a job seeker can actually see.

What I found

  • Postings without a company logo were fraudulent 15.9% of the time, about 8 times the rate of those with one.
  • Remote postings were fraudulent at 8.3%, nearly double the 4.7% rate for on-site roles.
  • Among the 10 countries with the most postings, Australia had the highest fraud rate at 18.7%.
  • Salary range was missing from 84% of postings, so I flagged it as too sparse to rely on.

A reservation database for a car rental company

We designed a relational database to run Hertz-style rental operations: cars, customers, loyalty members, reservations, locations, employees, billing, and add-ons. We wrote the schema, data dictionary, sample data, and the queries managers would need.

13linked tables
10business-question queries
Views + triggerfor billing logic

Sample query: what goes into a reservation's total?

-- Itemized bill: car rate, protection plan and add-ons, times rental length
SELECT b.BillID, r.ResID,
       s.CurrentRate AS carRate,
       p.Rate AS protectionPlanRate,
       DATEDIFF(r.DropoffDate, r.PickupDate) AS resLength,
       ((s.CurrentRate + p.Rate + COALESCE(ads.PerDayRate,0))
          * DATEDIFF(r.DropoffDate, r.PickupDate))
          + COALESCE(ads.FlatRate,0) AS totalCost
FROM Billing b
JOIN ProtectionPlan p ON b.PlanID = p.PlanID
JOIN Reservation r    ON b.ResID = r.ResID
JOIN Stationed s      ON r.VIN = s.VIN
LEFT JOIN (SELECT ServAddonID,
     SUM(CASE WHEN PricingType = 'Per Day'   THEN Price ELSE 0 END) AS PerDayRate,
     SUM(CASE WHEN PricingType = 'Flat Rate' THEN Price ELSE 0 END) AS FlatRate
   FROM AddOnServices GROUP BY ServAddonID) ads
  ON b.ServAddonID = ads.ServAddonID;

Simplified from our full itemized-bill view, which also adds physical add-ons like car seats.

When a company gets hacked, what should leaders do?

Data work comes with responsibility for the data. In these two cases I judged how leadership handled real security crises and what they should have done differently.

Target, 2013 breachArgued that Target's exposure came from inaction before and after the attack, looking at it from the consumer, legal, and technical side, including how mixed messages about PIN data left customers unprotected.
